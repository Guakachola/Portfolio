
## Establishing the Presidency

Established in Article II, executive power vested in POTUS. 
	Known as the 'vesting cause'
Article II empowers the chief executive to 'preserve, protect and defend the Constitution of the United States'. This also assumes the president as head of state. 

Presidents are: Natural born citizens; 35+; 14+ Years living in U.S.; (and through the 22nd amendment, terms last 4 years and cannot be elected more than twice).

President is commander in chief, which means he commands the national military and the National Guard units.
	They also head the CIA, NSC, NSA, FBI, and any other intelligence network.
He can deploy troops if they consider them necessary, i.e. for a disaster, emergency, or and need to maintain order.

The pres also makes treats (with advice and consent of the Senate). This is diplomatic actions. He can recognize countries (acknowledge their legitimacy) and can restore diplomatic ties. Forge **Executive Agreements** which are agreements made between the pres and another country.

The pres can also veto, which he doesn't use all the time, but he can threaten to veto if he wishes. He can also **Pocket Veto**, which is an automatic veto if the president ignores legislation.

The president also has Implied powers, which come the the 'necessary and proper' clause.

The unitary theory suggests that the president is a sovereign who is subject to few restraints.
	He Controls ass policy. The basis for 'regulatory review'. Critics say that it ignores checks and balances

## Cabinet

The cabinet is composed of the secretaries and chief administrators of major departments of the federal government
	Has no constitutional status
	Meets, buts makes no divisions as a group
	Appoints must be approved by the Senate
	Each of the 15 departments is lead by a secretary, which is the leader.
